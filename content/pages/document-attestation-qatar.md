Qatar will not accept most foreign documents until they have been attested. Skip a step and the document is returned. Official bodies also work in Arabic, so many papers need an accurate Arabic version. We handle both, in the right order, from your home country to the Ministry of Foreign Affairs in Doha.

## What gets attested

Personal documents:

- Educational certificates, degrees, diplomas, transcripts
- Birth, marriage and death certificates
- Experience and employment certificates
- Police clearance certificates

Company documents:

- Certificate of incorporation, memorandum and articles
- Board resolutions and powers of attorney
- Commercial invoices and certificates of origin
- Agreements and authorisation letters

## How the chain usually works

For a document issued outside Qatar, the usual order is:

1. The issuing office or a notary confirms the document.
2. The foreign ministry of the home country, or its designated office, attests it.
3. The Qatar embassy or consulate in that country attests it.
4. The Ministry of Foreign Affairs in Qatar attests it for use here.

Countries differ. Since late 2024, the Ministry of Foreign Affairs has also offered an online service to complete attestation for documents issued abroad. We use whichever route fits your country and your document. ## Documents issued in Qatar

Documents issued in Qatar that must be used abroad go through the Ministry of Foreign Affairs here, and then the embassy of the country where you will use them. We handle both ends.

## Educational certificates

For degrees and certificates, the university needs to be on the list recognised by Qatar's education ministry. We check before you spend money. Some roles and visas also ask for verification from the university or a third party. We tell you what the job or visa needs.

## Translation (legal translation) in Qatar

Authorities in Qatar work in Arabic. A wrong word in a contract or a company paper can hold up a filing or change its meaning. We provide certified legal translation between Arabic and English by qualified translators. [CONFIRM: other languages the client or partner can really cover]

We translate memoranda and articles, powers of attorney, board resolutions, contracts, court papers, certificates, licences and records, and documents for visas and ministry filings.

For official use, a translation often has to come from a licensed translation office and carry its stamp. We provide that and keep the layout of the original so stamps and signatures sit in the right place.

## Attestation and translation together

For foreign documents, the order matters. Usually the document is attested first and then translated, and sometimes the translation also needs attesting. We tell you the order so you do not pay twice.

**Q: Is notarisation the same as attestation?**

No. A notary confirms a signature or a copy. Attestation is a chain of official confirmations that makes the document valid in another country.

**Q: Does every translation have to be certified?**

For ministries, courts and most official filings, yes. For internal use a plain translation may be enough.

**Q: What if a ministry rejects my document?**

We find out why. Often it is a missing earlier step or a name mismatch. We fix it and resubmit.
