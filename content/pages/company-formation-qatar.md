Starting a company in Qatar follows a fixed order of steps. Get the first one wrong and everything after it waits. We choose the right route for your business, prepare the papers and submit them to the ministries. This page explains your options in plain words so you know what to ask for.

## Pick the type of company first

Everything else depends on this choice. Here is the short version.

- Mainland LLC. For businesses that sell to customers anywhere in Qatar. The most common choice. See: [LLC in Qatar](/company-formation-qatar/llc/)
- Free zone company. For export, storage, light industry, logistics and technology services.
- Qatar Financial Centre (QFC) company. For financial and professional services.
- Representative office. For a foreign company that wants to study the market, not trade.
- E-commerce licence. For people who sell online.

Already have a company and want to sell or change partners? See: Company share sale and transfer below.

If you are a foreign investor, also read: [100 percent foreign ownership](/company-formation-qatar/100-percent-foreign-ownership/)

## Free zone company in Qatar

Qatar has free zones run by the Qatar Free Zones Authority. The two main sites are Ras Bufontas, near Hamad International Airport, and Umm Alhoul, near Hamad Port. They suit exporters, re-exporters, businesses that store or process goods, manufacturers, and technology and logistics firms.

Foreign ownership is open inside the zones. The trade off is that a free zone company has limits on selling directly to customers on the mainland. If most of your buyers are in Doha shops, offices and homes, a mainland LLC usually fits better. We compare both for you and give two quotes, not just the cheaper one.

## Qatar Financial Centre (QFC) company setup

The QFC is an onshore business and financial centre in Doha. It has its own legal framework based on common law, its own regulator and its own tax regime. It fits banks, insurers, asset managers, advisers, and some holding or headquarters companies of international groups.

It is not built for shops, trading companies or contractors. Full foreign ownership is allowed. QFC entities file tax through the QFC systems, not the general Dhareeba portal, so the rules differ from the mainland.

## Representative office in Qatar

A representative office lets a foreign company have a presence in Qatar without opening a trading company. It is set up under Ministerial Decision No. 396 of 2017 through the Ministry of Commerce and Industry.

It can promote the parent company's products, meet customers and officials, study the market and act as a contact point. It cannot sign sales contracts in its own name, invoice customers or earn income in Qatar. If you want to sell, you need an LLC or a free zone company. Many clients open a representative office first and an LLC once the market looks worth it.

Foreign parent company documents need attestation and Arabic translation. See: [document attestation and translation](/document-attestation-qatar/)

## E-commerce licence in Qatar

Anyone who sells regularly online needs a licence. That includes websites, apps and shops run on Instagram, Facebook or WhatsApp as a regular business. Unlicensed sellers risk fines, closed pages and blocked payment accounts.

The e-commerce licence is issued through the Single Window platform with a commercial registration, and it lists what you may sell. Food, cosmetics, supplements and medical products need extra approval. Some online activities have lighter premises rules than a shop, but not all. ## Trading company setup in Qatar

Trading is the most common reason people open a company here. The trick is the activity description. Ministries do not accept a vague name like "General Trading" on its own. Each product group has its own activity code, and the list on your CR and licence decides what you may sell.

Ask for the activities you plan to sell in the first two years, not everything you might ever sell. Food, medical items, chemicals and regulated goods need extra approval. To import, your company also needs customs registration. [CONFIRM: whether the client handles customs registration directly or through a partner]

Most trading companies are LLCs. Premises must suit the goods, and the lease must be in order before the licence is issued.

## Service company setup in Qatar

A service company earns by doing work for clients. Consultants, IT firms, marketing agencies, cleaning and maintenance contractors and event organisers all fall here. Most register as a mainland LLC so they can serve clients anywhere in Qatar.

Some activities, such as education, health and engineering consultancy, need approval from another authority before the licence. Service firms are people businesses, so plan visas early. The number of visas depends on the company's size, premises and approvals. See: [visa services](/visa-services-qatar/)

## Do you need a Qatari partner or sponsor?

For most sectors, no. Under Law No. 1 of 2019, foreign investors can own up to 100 percent in most sectors with approval from the Ministry of Commerce and Industry. See: [100 percent foreign ownership](/company-formation-qatar/100-percent-foreign-ownership/)

Some owners still choose a Qatari partner for business reasons: a restricted sector, contracts where local participation helps, or a partner with market contacts. The word "sponsor" is also used for the person responsible for an employee's residence, which is a different thing.

If you take a partner, write the share split, roles, profit share and exit terms in a partnership agreement and have a lawyer read it. Never hand over a licence or bank access on a handshake.

## Company share sale and transfer in Qatar

Selling shares, buying into a company or changing partners is one of the most common company changes here. Signing a sale agreement is not the end. The new ownership only counts once the formalities are done and the company records are updated.

For a mainland LLC, the usual order is:

1. Check the memorandum of association. It may restrict transfers or give existing partners a right to buy first.
2. Give existing partners the chance to use that right, and keep their written reply or waiver.
3. Pass the shareholders' resolution approving the sale.
4. Apply to the General Tax Authority for approval of the share sale. A tax clearance is needed first, and capital gains tax can apply to the sale. The draft sale agreement is part of that application.
5. Sign the sale agreement and have it authenticated before the Ministry of Justice notary.
6. Amend the memorandum and update the commercial registration through Single Window.
7. Update the licence, establishment card, bank signatories and the beneficial owner records.

If a seller or buyer is a foreign company or a person abroad, their documents need attestation and Arabic translation. See: [document attestation and translation](/document-attestation-qatar/)

Private shareholding companies and QFC companies follow different procedures. Foreign ownership limits for the activity still apply to the buyer. See: [100 percent foreign ownership](/company-formation-qatar/100-percent-foreign-ownership/)

We prepare the resolutions, the partner notices and the agreements for your lawyer to review, handle the tax authority and Single Window filings, and update every record afterwards. For a typical mainland transfer, allow several weeks because of the tax clearance and ministry approval. [CONFIRM: client's usual range, and whether a lawyer drafts the sale agreement]

We are not a law firm. For a large sale, a dispute between partners or a company with debts, you should also have a lawyer.

## The steps on the mainland

1. Reserve a trade name through Single Window.
2. Prepare the memorandum of association and sign it, digitally or before a notary.
3. Register the company and receive the commercial registration (CR).
4. Get the commercial licence or permit for your activity.
5. Receive the establishment registration card.
6. Register with the General Tax Authority and get the tax card through Dhareeba.
7. Open a corporate bank account.
8. Apply for work visas and Qatar IDs for staff.

A CR alone does not allow you to trade. You need the licence too. Some activities need an extra approval before step 4, so we check this on day one.

## Corporate bank account opening in Qatar

A company cannot run without one. Banks usually ask for the CR and licence, the memorandum, the establishment card and tax card, a resolution naming signatories, and passports and Qatar IDs of owners. Minimum balances differ by bank. Salaries go through the Wage Protection System. Delays come from mismatched name spellings, unclear ownership, a missing tax card or an address the bank cannot confirm. We prepare the full set before you apply and follow up with the bank. Banks decide for themselves, and we tell you early if one is likely to say no.

## Questions we hear often

**Q: Can I own the company 100 percent as a foreigner?**

In most sectors, yes, with approval from the Ministry of Commerce and Industry. Some sectors are restricted. See our page on 100 percent foreign ownership.

**Q: How much capital do I need?**

For most LLCs there is no fixed general minimum. Some activities and banks have their own expectations. The old figure of QAR 200,000 is no longer the rule. We tell you what your activity needs.

**Q: Do I need an office?**

For most mainland licences, yes, an address that fits the activity. Some online activities have lighter options. We check what applies.
